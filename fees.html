Guide · For launchers

Fees and who gets what

There are two fees. The platform fee is fixed and the same for every launch. The creator fee is set by the launcher when they launch. Both are only taken if a sale sells out.

WhoGets
The platform5% of the ETH the sale keeps, after any price differences have been set aside for buyers
The launcherThe creator fee: a share of the liquidity reserve, in tokens, set at launch between 0% and 20% of the reserve
The poolThe remaining 95% of the ETH and the rest of the reserve tokens
BuyersExactly the tokens they bought, plus any ETH owed back from their batch's final price. Fees never reduce either.

The creator fee

The launcher picks their creator fee when they launch. It's a share of the liquidity reserve, the half of the supply held back for the pool, paid in tokens when the sale sells out. It can't be changed after launch.

Creator fee (share of the reserve)Share of total supplyTokens
0%0%None
5%2.5%25 million
10%5%50 million
20%, the most allowed10%100 million

The tokens come out of the pool's half, never out of what buyers bought. A higher creator fee means fewer tokens in the pool, which also lifts the price trading opens at. The pool at sellout explains that.

The launcher gets no ETH

Worth saying plainly: launching doesn't pay you in ETH. Your share is your creator fee, in tokens, which you receive when the sale sells out. Everything else goes into the market or the platform fee.

If the sale doesn't sell out

No fees are taken at all. Buyers withdraw exactly what they paid.

Other costs

There may be a fee for creating a token. If there is, the launch form shows it before you sign. On the alpha it's free. Network gas applies to every transaction, as it does anywhere on-chain, and the app shows an estimate before you sign.
