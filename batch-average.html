Batch-average pricing

Launchers choose between two ways of working out how many tokens each buyer gets. Most of the time you won't notice the difference, but it's worth knowing which one a sale uses.

ModeWhat it means
Pay at buy timeYou get the tokens your ETH bought at the price when you bought. This is the default.
Batch averageEveryone who buys in the same batch is allocated at that batch's average price.

When it makes a difference

Only when the price decays inside a batch. If there's no decay, everyone in a batch pays the same price anyway, so both modes give the same result.

With decay, batch average evens out the difference between buying early and late in the same batch. Nobody loses out for being first.

What you see while a batch is open

In a batch-average sale, the token amount shown when you buy is an estimate. It's final once the batch closes and the average is known. Your ETH is committed either way, and the refund rules are the same.

The price cap works on the quote

In a batch-average sale, the price cap on your buy limits the price quoted when your buy lands. It doesn't limit the batch average you end up being allocated at.

By the time claims open every batch has closed, so the amount you claim is always final.
